What's wrong with my plant?

Houseplant problems, by what you can see

Last updated 24 September 2026

Most houseplant problems show up as one of a handful of symptoms, and most of those come down to water, light or dry air. Each guide here starts from what you can see, shows how to read the pattern — which leaves, how fast, what the compost feels like — and then lists every plant in the PlantParlour care library where that symptom is a known problem, with its own cause and fix.

  • Why are my plant's leaves turning yellow?

    Most often, overwatering.

  • Why are my plant's leaf tips turning brown?

    Brown, crispy tips and edges mean the far end of the leaf lost water faster than the roots could replace it — because the air is dry, the compost dried out too far, or salts and fluoride from tap water and fertiliser have built up in the pot.

  • Spider mites on houseplants: how to spot them and get rid of them

    Spider mites are tiny sap-sucking mites, barely visible without a magnifier, that thrive in warm, dry indoor air.

  • Why is my plant leggy? Stretched stems and bare vines

    A leggy plant — long bare stems, leaves spaced far apart, growth leaning toward the window — is almost always short of light, and stretching to find more.

  • Why is my plant dropping leaves?

    Leaf drop is a plant cutting its losses.

  • Why is my variegated plant turning green?

    White, cream, pink or silver parts of a leaf have little or no chlorophyll, so they cost the plant energy it can only afford in good light.
