Calathea orbifolia (Goeppertia orbifolia) care

Prayer plants · fussy to keep

Calathea orbifolia (Goeppertia orbifolia) wants medium indirect light; never direct sun. Water it every 5 days. Keep evenly damp — never soggy, never dry — and use rainwater or filtered water if you can. Tap water is the most common cause of the brown edges this family is famous for. Pot it in peat-free compost with perlite, moisture-retentive but not heavy. A balanced feed at quarter to half strength monthly in spring and summer. They are sensitive to over-feeding, and repot once a year. Humidity: above 60%. Below that, the edges brown and it will not stop. Non-toxic to cats and dogs — the whole family is safe, which is much of their appeal.

Big round leaves, up to 30 cm across, banded in pale silver-green stripes — the calathea most people want and the one that punishes tap water hardest. It was moved from Calathea to Goeppertia in 2012, and nobody selling it has noticed. Rainwater and 60% humidity are not optional here.

Watering a Calathea orbifolia

Water every 5 days. Keep evenly damp — never soggy, never dry — and use rainwater or filtered water if you can. Tap water is the most common cause of the brown edges this family is famous for.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Medium indirect light. Calatheas — which is what most of these were called until the genus was split, and what everyone still calls them — burn in direct sun and lose their markings in a dark corner.

Humidity and temperature

Above 60%. Below that, the edges brown and it will not stop. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

A light, moisture-retentive mix — compost with perlite and a little bark. The roots are fine and shallow, so a wide shallow pot beats a deep one.

Feeding

A balanced feed at quarter to half strength monthly in spring and summer. They are sensitive to over-feeding. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Every spring, into a shallow pot. Divide the clump at the same time if it has spread.

Common problems

  • Brown crispy edges on every leaf

    Dry air, or minerals in tap water. Usually both.

    Fix: Get the humidity above 60% and switch to rainwater or filtered water. Existing damage will not heal.

  • Leaves curling inward

    Thirsty, or the air is too dry.

    Fix: Water and raise the humidity; the leaves uncurl within a day if that was it.

  • Leaves staying folded up all day

    Stress — too dry, too cold, or too dark.

    Fix: The folding is normal at night. All day means something is wrong: check water, warmth and light in that order.

  • Fine webbing under the leaves

    Spider mites, which love this family.

    Fix: Shower the plant and treat weekly for a month; raise the humidity or they return.

Propagation

Divide the clump at repotting, giving each piece roots and several leaves. Stem cuttings with a node also root in water.
