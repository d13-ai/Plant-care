Pencil cactus (Euphorbia tirucalli) care

Succulents & cacti · easy to keep

Pencil cactus (Euphorbia tirucalli) wants bright light with direct sun. Water it every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then leave it until the pot is bone dry and the lower leaves have gone slightly soft. Never water into the rosette — water sitting in the centre rots it. Pot it in gritty cactus compost, at least half grit or perlite. A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: indifferent; dry air is ideal. Toxic to cats, dogs and people, and the most genuinely hazardous plant in the house.

Pencil cactus, or firesticks: a tangle of smooth green pencil-thick stems with almost no leaves, turning coral-orange in strong sun and cold. Also not a cactus, and carrying the same dangerous latex as its milk-tree relative — this is the plant to be most careful with in the house.

Watering a Pencil cactus

Water every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then leave it until the pot is bone dry and the lower leaves have gone slightly soft. Never water into the rosette — water sitting in the centre rots it.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

As much direct sun as you can give it — four to six hours minimum. An echeveria in a normal room stretches into a pale tower within weeks, and no amount of later light will make it compact again.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent; dry air is ideal. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Cactus compost cut with more grit. A terracotta pot helps because it wicks water out of the mix and away from the roots.

Feeding

A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every two or three years in spring, into a pot barely larger than the rosette.

Common problems

  • Stretched, pale, spaced-out leaves

    Not enough light. This is not reversible.

    Fix: Cut the head off, let it callus for a few days, and re-root it in full sun. The stump usually produces offsets.

    Leggy, stretched growth: how to read it on any plant

  • Mushy translucent lower leaves

    Overwatering.

    Fix: Stop watering. Remove the affected leaves and let it dry out completely.

  • Shrivelled wrinkled leaves

    Genuinely thirsty.

    Fix: Water thoroughly and it plumps back up within a day or two.

  • White cottony blobs between the leaves

    Mealybugs.

    Fix: Alcohol on a cotton bud, every few days for a month — they hide deep in the rosette.

Propagation

Stem cuttings, wearing gloves. Dip the cut end in water to stop the latex, let it callus for several days, then root in dry gritty mix.

Toxicity and safety

Toxic to cats, dogs and people, and the most genuinely hazardous plant in the house. The white latex burns skin and can cause lasting damage to eyes — wear gloves and eye protection when cutting one, and wash immediately if it touches you.

The ASPCA lists Pencil Cactus (Euphorbia tirucalli) as toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it.
