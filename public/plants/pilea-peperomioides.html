Chinese money plant (Pilea peperomioides) care

Vines & trailers · easy to keep

Chinese money plant (Pilea peperomioides) wants bright indirect light. Water it weekly. Let the top half of the pot dry out, then water thoroughly. The leaves store water — a peperomia would much rather be dry. Pot it in light and free-draining — compost with plenty of perlite, or an aroid mix. A weak balanced feed monthly in spring and summer, and repot once a year. Humidity: room humidity is fine for most; the trailing kinds prefer above 60%. Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The Chinese money plant: round coin-shaped leaves on long stalks radiating from a central stem. It was carried out of Yunnan by a Norwegian missionary in 1946 and spread through Scandinavia entirely by cuttings passed between friends, decades before it reached the trade. It pups freely, which is why.

Watering a Chinese money plant

Water weekly. Let the top half of the pot dry out, then water thoroughly. The leaves store water — a peperomia would much rather be dry.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light, and turn the pot weekly — a pilea leans hard toward its light source and goes permanently lopsided otherwise.

Humidity and temperature

Room humidity is fine for most; the trailing kinds prefer above 60%. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Peperomias have small, shallow root systems and semi-succulent leaves. A light, airy mix in a small pot is the whole secret; a big pot of wet compost is the whole problem.

Feeding

A weak balanced feed monthly in spring and summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Rarely, and into a pot barely bigger than the last. Peperomias are happiest tight.

Common problems

  • Leaning heavily to one side

    It grows toward light faster than most.

    Fix: Turn the pot a quarter turn every week.

  • Yellow lower leaves dropping

    Overwatering, or simply age as the stem lengthens.

    Fix: Let it dry out more between waterings; a bare lower stem on an older pilea is normal.

  • Curling or cupping leaves

    Usually too much light; sometimes too little water.

    Fix: Move it out of direct sun and check the compost.

  • No babies

    Too young, or not enough light.

    Fix: Give it a bright spot and wait — pileas pup freely once established.

Propagation

Pot up the babies that come up from the soil around the parent once they have a few leaves; cut them off below soil level with a little root attached.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

Named cultivars and variegations

Pilea peperomioides is sold under several cultivar names. Care is the same unless noted; variegated forms carry less chlorophyll, so they grow more slowly and want more light than the plain species.
