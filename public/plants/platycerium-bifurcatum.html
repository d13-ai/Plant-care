Staghorn fern (Platycerium bifurcatum) care

Ferns · straightforward to keep

Staghorn fern (Platycerium bifurcatum) wants bright indirect light. Water it weekly. Soak the whole mount or root ball in water for ten minutes, then let it drain and dry out somewhat before the next soak — usually weekly. Pot it in none, ideally — mounted on bark with a pad of sphagnum moss. A weak balanced feed monthly in spring and summer, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: above 60%. A fern in dry air is a fern on its way out. Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The staghorn fern: two kinds of frond — flat round shield fronds that clasp the mount and brown as they age, and forked antler fronds that hang out from it. It is an epiphyte and is happiest mounted on a board with sphagnum rather than in a pot of compost.

Watering a Staghorn fern

Water weekly. Soak the whole mount or root ball in water for ten minutes, then let it drain and dry out somewhat before the next soak — usually weekly.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Medium indirect light, and never direct sun. A bright north window or a well-lit bathroom is close to ideal.

Humidity and temperature

Above 60%. A fern in dry air is a fern on its way out. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Staghorns are epiphytes that grow on tree trunks. Mounted on a board with sphagnum behind them is how they are happiest; in a pot, use pure bark and moss rather than compost.

Feeding

A weak balanced feed monthly in spring and summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every spring. Divide the clump at the same time if it has filled the pot.

Common problems

  • The flat round shield frond turning brown

    Normal. Shield fronds brown and stay on as the plant's anchor.

    Fix: Leave it exactly where it is — removing it damages the plant.

  • Black spots spreading on the antler fronds

    Too wet, or water sitting on the fronds in still air.

    Fix: Water at the base, improve airflow, and let it dry between soaks.

Propagation

Separate the pups that form at the base of a mature plant, each with a piece of the shield frond.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs.
