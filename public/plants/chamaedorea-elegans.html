Parlour palm (Chamaedorea elegans) care

Palms · easy to keep

Parlour palm (Chamaedorea elegans) wants medium to low indirect light. Water it weekly. Keep the top of the compost just barely damp. Palms will not forgive a pot left standing in water, and brown tips are the receipt for the one time you let it dry out hard. Pot it in standard compost with perlite. A weak balanced feed monthly in spring and summer only, and repot every 1.5 years. Humidity: above 50% keeps the tips green. Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The parlour palm — named for the Victorian drawing rooms it survived, on gaslight and coal dust. Slender green canes with soft arching fronds, tolerant of low light and cool rooms, and non-toxic to cats and dogs. What is sold as one plant is usually a dozen seedlings sharing a pot.

Watering a Parlour palm

Water weekly. Keep the top of the compost just barely damp. Palms will not forgive a pot left standing in water, and brown tips are the receipt for the one time you let it dry out hard.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Medium indirect light, and genuinely tolerant of less — the parlour palm earned its name in Victorian drawing rooms lit by gaslight. Direct sun scorches it.

Humidity and temperature

Above 50% keeps the tips green. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Ordinary peat-free compost with perlite through it. Palms want moisture-retentive but never waterlogged.

Feeding

A weak balanced feed monthly in spring and summer only. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 1.5 years. Every two or three years, and reluctantly — palm roots resent disturbance. What is sold as one plant is usually a dozen seedlings in a pot; keep them together.

Common problems

  • Brown tips on every frond

    Dry air, hard tap water, or a dry spell. Almost universal indoors.

    Fix: Raise humidity, water with rainwater, and trim the brown off with scissors following the leaf's own shape.

  • Spider mites

    Dry warm air. Palms are a favourite.

    Fix: Shower the fronds, both sides, and treat weekly for a month.

  • Whole fronds yellowing from the bottom

    Normal ageing if one at a time; overwatering if several.

    Fix: Cut old fronds off at the base and check the mix is not staying wet.

Propagation

From seed only. A clump can be split, but each piece is an individual seedling and splitting often kills several.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.
