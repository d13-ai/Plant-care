Panda plant (Kalanchoe tomentosa) care

Succulents & cacti · easy to keep

Last updated 15 October 2026

Panda plant (Kalanchoe tomentosa) wants bright light with some direct sun. Water it every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then leave it until the pot is bone dry and the lower leaves have gone slightly soft. Never water into the rosette — water sitting in the centre rots it. Pot it in gritty cactus compost, at least half grit or perlite. A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: indifferent; dry air is ideal. Toxic to cats and dogs.

Panda plant: upright stems of thick, felted, grey-green leaves edged with rust-brown spots, soft as a cat's ear. A slow, easy succulent grown for its leaves — it rarely flowers indoors — and, like every kalanchoe, toxic to pets.

Watering a Panda plant

Water every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then leave it until the pot is bone dry and the lower leaves have gone slightly soft. Never water into the rosette — water sitting in the centre rots it.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

As much direct sun as you can give it — four to six hours minimum. An echeveria in a normal room stretches into a pale tower within weeks, and no amount of later light will make it compact again.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent; dry air is ideal. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Cactus compost cut with more grit. A terracotta pot helps because it wicks water out of the mix and away from the roots.

Feeding

A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every two or three years in spring, into a pot barely larger than the rosette.

Common problems

  • Stretched leggy growth

    Not enough light.

    Fix: Move it into direct sun, and cut it back in spring.

  • Mushy stem at the base

    Overwatering.

    Fix: Take healthy cuttings and start again.

Propagation

Twist a whole leaf cleanly off the stem, lay it on dry compost, and wait — roots and a tiny rosette form in a few weeks. Beheading a stretched plant and re-rooting the top also works.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Toxic to cats and dogs. Kalanchoes contain cardiac glycosides, which affect the heart — this is one of the more seriously toxic houseplants sold, and worth keeping out of reach.

The ASPCA lists Kalanchoe (Kalanchoe spp), the genus as a whole, as toxic to cats and dogs (checked 15 October 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 15 October 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.
