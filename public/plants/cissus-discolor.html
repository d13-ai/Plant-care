Rex begonia vine (Cissus discolor) care

Vines & trailers · fussy to keep

Rex begonia vine (Cissus discolor) wants bright indirect light. Water it every 5 days. Keep evenly damp. It wilts dramatically when dry and the leaves often do not fully recover. Pot it in airy compost with bark and perlite. Balanced feed at half strength every second or third watering in the growing season, and repot once a year. Humidity: above 60%. It will not thrive in dry air, full stop. The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to cats and dogs.

Rex begonia vine: velvety leaves quilted in silver, green and deep pink with red undersides, on a fast climbing vine with curling tendrils. It looks like a begonia and is actually a grape relative. It is beautiful and it is a humidity plant — below about 60% it crisps and drops.

Watering a Rex begonia vine

Water every 5 days. Keep evenly damp. It wilts dramatically when dry and the leaves often do not fully recover.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light, no direct sun. This is a jungle vine and the leaves mark easily.

Humidity and temperature

Above 60%. It will not thrive in dry air, full stop. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

An airy mix with bark and perlite. It climbs, and the roots want air as much as water.

Feeding

Balanced feed at half strength every second or third watering in the growing season. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Every spring while it is growing fast. Give it something to climb.

Common problems

  • Crispy edges and leaf drop

    Dry air. This is the whole story with this plant.

    Fix: Get it above 60% humidity — a cabinet or terrarium is the honest answer.

    Brown tips and edges: how to read it on any plant

  • Powdery white coating

    Mildew from still, damp air.

    Fix: Improve the airflow; humidity without circulation is what causes it.

Propagation

Node cuttings root in water or sphagnum within a few weeks.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The ASPCA lists Begonia, Climbing (Cissus dicolor) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.

Questions people ask

How often should you water a Rex begonia vine?

Water every 5 days as a starting point — that is the cadence PlantParlour sets for Cissus discolor. Keep evenly damp. It wilts dramatically when dry and the leaves often do not fully recover. Light, pot size and the time of year all move that number, so treat it as a reminder to go and check rather than a rule.

How much light does a Rex begonia vine need?

Bright indirect light, no direct sun. This is a jungle vine and the leaves mark easily.

Is Rex begonia vine toxic to cats and dogs?

Non-toxic to cats and dogs. The ASPCA lists Begonia, Climbing (Cissus dicolor) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026). If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435 straight away.
