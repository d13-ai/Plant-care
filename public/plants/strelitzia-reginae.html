Orange bird of paradise (Strelitzia reginae) care

Trees · straightforward to keep

Orange bird of paradise (Strelitzia reginae) wants the brightest spot you have, with several hours of direct sun. Water it weekly. Water thoroughly when the top third is dry. They drink hard in summer and much less in winter. Pot it in rich, loam-based, free-draining compost. A balanced feed every two weeks through spring and summer — they are heavy feeders, and repot every 1.5 years. Humidity: room humidity is fine. Toxic to cats and dogs — the seeds and flowers most of all.

The orange bird of paradise: smaller and stiffer than nicolai, with grey-green paddle leaves and — given enough sun and enough age — the famous orange and blue crane-headed flower. It needs four or five years and the brightest window in the house before it will bloom.

Watering an Orange bird of paradise

Water weekly. Water thoroughly when the top third is dry. They drink hard in summer and much less in winter.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

As much light as possible, including direct sun. A bird of paradise will not flower in anything less, and indoors most never do — treat the leaves as the point and any flower as a bonus.

Humidity and temperature

Room humidity is fine. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

A rich loam-based compost with perlite or grit. These are big hungry plants with thick fleshy roots that still resent standing water.

Feeding

A balanced feed every two weeks through spring and summer — they are heavy feeders. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 1.5 years. Every second spring while young, then leave it: strelitzias flower better potbound, and a mature one is heavy enough that top-dressing is the practical option.

Common problems

  • Leaves splitting along the ribs

    Normal. The splits let wind through in the wild.

    Fix: Nothing to fix. Splits appear faster in a draught or when the leaves rub against something.

  • No flowers after years indoors

    Not enough light, too much pot, or simply not old enough — they flower at four or five years.

    Fix: Give it the sunniest position you have, feed through summer, and stop repotting.

  • Brown crispy leaf edges

    Under-watering or salt build-up.

    Fix: Water more thoroughly and flush the pot a few times a year.

Propagation

Divide a large clump in spring, giving each piece several leaves and a good share of root. Seed is very slow.

Toxicity and safety

Toxic to cats and dogs — the seeds and flowers most of all. Causes vomiting and drowsiness.
