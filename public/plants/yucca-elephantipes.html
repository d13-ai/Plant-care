Spineless yucca (Yucca elephantipes) care

Trees · easy to keep

Spineless yucca (Yucca elephantipes) wants as much direct sun as you can give it. Water it every 2 weeks. Water when the pot is completely dry, and sparingly in winter. A yucca will go a month without complaint. Pot it in gritty and sharply drained — cactus compost. A weak feed two or three times a year, no more, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: indifferent; dry air suits it. Mildly toxic to cats and dogs; the saponins cause vomiting.

The spineless yucca: thick woody canes topped with fountains of sword-shaped leaves, sold as sawn-off logs that sprout. It wants the sunniest spot in the house and almost no water — indoors it is nearly always overwatered, and the base goes soft long before the top shows anything.

Watering a Spineless yucca

Water every 2 weeks. Water when the pot is completely dry, and sparingly in winter. A yucca will go a month without complaint.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Full sun. A yucca indoors wants the brightest window in the house and will stretch and weaken anywhere else.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent; dry air suits it. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Cactus compost, or ordinary compost cut with a third horticultural grit. The cane rots quickly in anything that holds water.

Feeding

A weak feed two or three times a year, no more. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every three years or so. They are top-heavy — use a wide, weighted pot.

Common problems

  • Soft brown base, leaves pulling away

    Rot from overwatering.

    Fix: Cut the cane back above the rot, let it callus for a few days, and re-root it in dry gritty mix.

  • Leaves going pale and floppy

    Not enough light.

    Fix: Move it to the sunniest window you have.

  • Brown leaf tips

    Dry air or fluoride in tap water.

    Fix: Trim them at an angle with scissors and switch to rainwater.

Propagation

Cut the cane into sections and root them in gritty compost, or pot up the offsets that appear at the base.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Mildly toxic to cats and dogs; the saponins cause vomiting. The leaf tips are sharp enough to be the greater hazard with small children.
