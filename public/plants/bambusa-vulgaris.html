Bamboo (Bambusa vulgaris) care

Other · needs attention to keep

Bamboo (Bambusa vulgaris) wants bright indirect light. Water it every 5 days. Keep the compost damp at all times. Bamboo in a pot dries out fast and drops leaves when it does. Pot it in rich, moisture-retentive compost. A high-nitrogen feed every two weeks in spring and summer, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: above 50%. Not on the ASPCA's list of toxic or non-toxic plants, so we don't call it pet-safe: not being listed is not the same as being tested.

Common bamboo: a true woody bamboo, fast, thirsty and demanding of light, which grows into a substantial clump in a large pot. Worth knowing that the 'lucky bamboo' sold in a vase of pebbles and water is not this plant at all — it is Dracaena sanderiana, a dracaena, and it is toxic to cats and dogs where true bamboo is not.

Watering a Bamboo

Water every 5 days. Keep the compost damp at all times. Bamboo in a pot dries out fast and drops leaves when it does.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light. True bamboos want brightness and airflow; they do poorly in a dark corner.

Humidity and temperature

Above 50%. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

A rich compost that holds water. Bamboo is thirsty and roots hard into whatever it is given.

Feeding

A high-nitrogen feed every two weeks in spring and summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every spring — bamboo fills a pot with root in a single season and will eventually split it.

Common problems

  • Leaves curling lengthways

    Thirsty.

    Fix: Water more often; potted bamboo needs it almost daily in summer.

  • Yellow leaves

    Overwatering in a pot with no drainage, or hard water.

    Fix: Check the pot drains; use rainwater.

    Yellow leaves: how to read it on any plant

  • Spider mites

    Dry indoor air.

    Fix: Raise humidity and treat weekly.

Propagation

Divide the clump in spring with a saw. It is heavy work.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Not on the ASPCA's list of toxic or non-toxic plants, so we don't call it pet-safe: not being listed is not the same as being tested. Keep it out of reach of a pet that chews.

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.

Questions people ask

Is lucky bamboo the same as real bamboo?

No. Lucky bamboo — the stems sold standing in pebbles and water, sometimes spiralled — is Dracaena sanderiana, a member of the asparagus family. True bamboos are grasses and will not grow in a vase of water. The practical difference that matters at home: lucky bamboo is toxic to cats and dogs, and true bamboo is not.

How often should you water a Bamboo?

Water every 5 days as a starting point — that is the cadence PlantParlour sets for Bambusa vulgaris. Keep the compost damp at all times. Bamboo in a pot dries out fast and drops leaves when it does. Light, pot size and the time of year all move that number, so treat it as a reminder to go and check rather than a rule.

How much light does a Bamboo need?

Bright indirect light. True bamboos want brightness and airflow; they do poorly in a dark corner.

Is Bamboo toxic to cats and dogs?

Not on the ASPCA's list of toxic or non-toxic plants, so we don't call it pet-safe: not being listed is not the same as being tested. Keep it out of reach of a pet that chews. If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435 straight away.

How do you propagate a Bamboo?

Divide the clump in spring with a saw. It is heavy work.
