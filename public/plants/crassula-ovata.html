Jade plant (Crassula ovata) care

Trees · easy to keep

Jade plant (Crassula ovata) wants full sun to bright indirect light. Water it every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then not again until the pot is bone dry and the leaves have lost a little of their firmness. In winter, once a month is plenty. Pot it in cactus compost with extra grit. A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: indifferent; dry air is fine. Toxic to cats and dogs; causes vomiting and lethargy.

The jade plant: fat glossy oval leaves on a thickening woody trunk, slowly becoming a miniature tree. Given full sun and very little water it lives for decades and takes on red-edged leaves and a genuinely gnarled shape. Given a shady corner and regular watering, it stretches and falls apart.

Watering a Jade plant

Water every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then not again until the pot is bone dry and the leaves have lost a little of their firmness. In winter, once a month is plenty.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Four to six hours of direct sun. A jade in a dim room stretches into a thin pale thing that never regains its compact shape.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent; dry air is fine. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Cactus compost, ideally with more grit still. The one thing a jade cannot survive is a mix that stays damp.

Feeding

A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every three or four years, and into a heavy pot — a mature jade is top-heavy and a plastic pot will tip.

Common problems

  • Leaves dropping at a touch

    Overwatering, almost always.

    Fix: Stop watering until the pot is fully dry, then water far less often.

    Leaves falling off: how to read it on any plant

  • Stretched pale stems with leaves far apart

    Not enough light.

    Fix: Move it into direct sun and cut the stretched growth back — the cuttings will root.

    Leggy, stretched growth: how to read it on any plant

  • Red edges on the leaves

    Sun and cool nights. This is a good sign, not a problem.

    Fix: Nothing to fix; it is the plant colouring up.

  • White cottony spots in the leaf joints

    Mealybugs.

    Fix: Dab each one with alcohol on a cotton bud and check again weekly for a month.

Propagation

A single leaf laid on dry compost roots and forms a plantlet in a few weeks. Stem cuttings work too — let the cut end callus for a couple of days first.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Toxic to cats and dogs; causes vomiting and lethargy. Not a hazard to people.

The ASPCA lists Jade Plant (Crassula argentea) as toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.

Named cultivars and variegations

Crassula ovata is sold under several cultivar names. Care is the same unless noted; variegated forms carry less chlorophyll, so they grow more slowly and want more light than the plain species.

Questions people ask

How often should you water a Jade plant?

Water every 2 weeks as a starting point — that is the cadence PlantParlour sets for Crassula ovata. Light, pot size and the time of year all move that number, so treat it as a reminder to go and check rather than a rule.
