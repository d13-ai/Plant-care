Swedish ivy (Plectranthus verticillatus) care

Vines & trailers · easy to keep

Last updated 15 October 2026

Swedish ivy (Plectranthus verticillatus) wants bright indirect light. Water it every 6 days. Keep the compost lightly damp, letting the top centimetre or two dry between waterings. Pot it in standard compost with perlite. A balanced feed at half strength monthly in spring and summer, and repot once a year. Humidity: room humidity is fine. The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to cats and dogs.

Swedish ivy — neither Swedish nor an ivy, but a fast, trailing relative of mint with round, scalloped, glossy leaves and spikes of small white flowers. An old-fashioned and very easy hanging-basket plant.

Watering a Swedish ivy

Water every 6 days. Keep the compost lightly damp, letting the top centimetre or two dry between waterings.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light, with a little morning sun. Swedish ivy stretches and drops its lower leaves in a dim spot.

Humidity and temperature

Room humidity is fine. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Ordinary potting compost with some perlite. It is a fast, forgiving plant.

Feeding

A balanced feed at half strength monthly in spring and summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Every spring, or restart it from cuttings once it gets leggy.

Common problems

  • Long bare stems

    Not enough light, and no pinching.

    Fix: Pinch the tips regularly to make it branch, and move it brighter.

  • Wilting and dropping leaves

    It dried out — or it sits wet.

    Fix: Check the compost: water if it is dry, let it drain if it is soggy.

    Leaves falling off: how to read it on any plant

  • White cottony blobs in the leaf joints

    Mealybugs.

    Fix: Dab them with alcohol on a cotton bud every few days for a month.

Propagation

Stem cuttings root in water within a week or two.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The ASPCA lists Swedish Ivy (Plectranthus australis) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 15 October 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 15 October 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.

Questions people ask

How often should you water a Swedish ivy?

Water every 6 days as a starting point — that is the cadence PlantParlour sets for Plectranthus verticillatus. Keep the compost lightly damp, letting the top centimetre or two dry between waterings. Light, pot size and the time of year all move that number, so treat it as a reminder to go and check rather than a rule.

How much light does a Swedish ivy need?

Bright indirect light, with a little morning sun. Swedish ivy stretches and drops its lower leaves in a dim spot.

Is Swedish ivy toxic to cats and dogs?

Non-toxic to cats and dogs. The ASPCA lists Swedish Ivy (Plectranthus australis) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 15 October 2026). If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435 straight away.

How do you propagate a Swedish ivy?

Stem cuttings root in water within a week or two.
