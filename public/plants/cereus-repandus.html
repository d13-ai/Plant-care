Peruvian apple cactus (Cereus repandus) care

Succulents & cacti · easy to keep

The Peruvian apple cactus: a tall ribbed blue-green column that grows steadily upward and is often sold as an architectural floor plant. It is straightforward and long-lived, and it gets heavy — put it in a substantial pot from the start.

Watering a Peruvian apple cactus

Water every 3 weeks. Water thoroughly in summer once the pot is completely dry, roughly every three weeks. From late autumn to early spring, stop almost entirely — a cold wet cactus is a dead cactus.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Full direct sun, as much as there is. Desert cacti etiolate into pale asparagus in anything less.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent; dry air is ideal. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Cactus compost with at least half grit or pumice, in a terracotta pot. Drainage is the entire discipline with cacti.

Feeding

A weak cactus feed two or three times over the summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every three or four years, into a heavy pot — a tall column cactus is top-heavy.

Common problems

  • Soft brown patches at the base

    Rot from water in cold conditions.

    Fix: Cut above the rot, callus the cut, and re-root. Keep it dry all winter.

  • Pale stretched narrow growth at the top

    Not enough light.

    Fix: Move it into full sun. The stretched part stays stretched.

    Leggy, stretched growth: how to read it on any plant

  • Corky brown patches low down

    Normal ageing on an older cactus.

    Fix: Nothing to fix.

Propagation

Detach a pad or an offset, let the cut callus for a week, then set it in dry gritty compost and wait a fortnight before watering.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Not on the ASPCA's list of toxic or non-toxic plants, so we don't call it pet-safe: not being listed is not the same as being tested. Keep it out of reach of a pet that chews. The spines are a hazard in their own right.

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.
