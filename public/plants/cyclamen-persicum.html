Cyclamen (Cyclamen persicum) care

Flowering · needs attention to keep

Cyclamen (Cyclamen persicum) wants bright indirect light, and cool. Water it every 6 days. Water from below, into the saucer, and never into the crown. Let it take up what it wants for twenty minutes, then pour the rest away. Pot it in free-draining compost with grit. A weak high-potash feed every two weeks while in flower, and repot once a year. Humidity: above 50%. Toxic to cats and dogs — the tuber especially, which can cause heart problems in quantity.

Cyclamen: swept-back flowers in white, pink and red over marbled heart-shaped leaves, flowering right through winter. It wants a cool room — 12–15 °C — and collapses in a warm one, which is why most are treated as disposable. Kept cool and rested dry through summer, the tuber comes back for years.

Watering a Cyclamen

Water every 6 days. Water from below, into the saucer, and never into the crown. Let it take up what it wants for twenty minutes, then pour the rest away.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light in a genuinely cool room — 12–15 °C. A cyclamen in a warm lounge collapses within a fortnight, which is why most are treated as disposable.

Humidity and temperature

Above 50%. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Free-draining compost with grit, and the top of the tuber left proud of the surface — buried, it rots.

Feeding

A weak high-potash feed every two weeks while in flower. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Repot the dormant tuber in late summer, keeping its shoulders above the compost.

Common problems

  • Collapsing, floppy leaves and stems

    Too warm — or water in the crown.

    Fix: Move it somewhere cool and water from below only.

  • Yellow leaves in spring

    Normal. It is going dormant.

    Fix: Stop watering, let it die back, and keep the tuber dry until late summer.

  • Grey fuzzy mould on the stems

    Botrytis from damp still air.

    Fix: Remove affected parts, improve airflow, and keep water off the crown.

Propagation

From seed. Tubers do not divide successfully.
