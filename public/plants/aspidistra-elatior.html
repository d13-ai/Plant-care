Cast iron plant (Aspidistra elatior) care

Other · easy to keep

Last updated 24 September 2026

Cast iron plant (Aspidistra elatior) wants low to medium indirect light. Water it every 12 days. Water when the top half of the pot is dry. It survives being forgotten for a month, which is the whole point of it. Pot it in standard compost with perlite. A weak feed two or three times over the summer. Over-feeding scorches the leaf tips, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: indifferent. The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The cast iron plant: broad dark leaves straight out of the compost, and the toughest genuinely low-light plant there is. It survived Victorian parlours lit by gas and heated by coal, which is where the name came from — and it is still the right answer for a dark hallway. Expect two or three new leaves a year and no drama.

Watering a Cast iron plant

Water every 12 days. Water when the top half of the pot is dry. It survives being forgotten for a month, which is the whole point of it.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Low to medium indirect light — it is called the cast iron plant for a reason, and it is the best genuine low-light plant on this list. Direct sun scorches it.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Ordinary compost with perlite. Nothing special required.

Feeding

A weak feed two or three times over the summer. Over-feeding scorches the leaf tips. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every four or five years. It is very slow and resents disturbance.

Common problems

  • Brown leaf tips

    Over-feeding, or hard water.

    Fix: Feed less and flush the pot with rainwater.

    Brown tips and edges: how to read it on any plant

  • Pale bleached patches

    Direct sun.

    Fix: Move it into shade.

  • Very slow growth

    Normal. Two or three new leaves a year is a good year.

    Fix: Nothing to fix; do not over-water trying to speed it up.

Propagation

Divide the rhizome in spring, giving each piece two or three leaves.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The ASPCA lists Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.

Questions people ask

How often should you water a Cast iron plant?

Water every 12 days as a starting point — that is the cadence PlantParlour sets for Aspidistra elatior. Water when the top half of the pot is dry. It survives being forgotten for a month, which is the whole point of it. Light, pot size and the time of year all move that number, so treat it as a reminder to go and check rather than a rule.

How much light does a Cast iron plant need?

Low to medium indirect light — it is called the cast iron plant for a reason, and it is the best genuine low-light plant on this list. Direct sun scorches it.

Is Cast iron plant toxic to cats and dogs?

Non-toxic to cats and dogs. The ASPCA lists Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026). If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435 straight away.
