Cape primrose (Streptocarpus hybridus) care

Begonias & violets · straightforward to keep

Cape primrose (Streptocarpus hybridus) wants bright indirect light; cooler rooms suit it. Water it every 5 days. Water from below: stand the pot in 2 cm of tepid water for twenty minutes, then drain. Cold water on the leaves leaves permanent pale rings. Pot it in a light African violet mix — peat-free compost with plenty of perlite. A high-phosphorus African violet feed at quarter strength with most waterings while in flower, and repot once a year. Humidity: above 50%. Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

Cape primrose: a relative of the African violet with long crinkled leaves and sprays of trumpet flowers held clear of the foliage, in violet, pink, white and near-black. It flowers for months, prefers a cooler room than most houseplants, and propagates from a leaf cut in half lengthways.

Watering a Cape primrose

Water every 5 days. Water from below: stand the pot in 2 cm of tepid water for twenty minutes, then drain. Cold water on the leaves leaves permanent pale rings.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light — an east window is close to perfect. African violets flower reliably at a light level that suits an ordinary room, which is much of why they have been popular for a century.

Humidity and temperature

Above 50%. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

A light open mix in a small shallow pot. African violets flower best when snug and rot if the crown sits in wet compost.

Feeding

A high-phosphorus African violet feed at quarter strength with most waterings while in flower. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Once a year, into the same size pot with fresh mix. Bury the bare stem a little deeper each time.

Common problems

  • Pale rings and blotches on the leaves

    Cold water splashed on the leaves.

    Fix: Water from below with tepid water. The marks are permanent on those leaves.

  • No flowers

    Too little light, too big a pot, or too much nitrogen.

    Fix: Move it to a bright east window, keep it snug, and use a high-phosphorus feed.

  • Crown going soft and brown

    Water sat in the centre of the rosette.

    Fix: Water from below only. A rotted crown is usually fatal — take leaf cuttings while you can.

Propagation

Cut a leaf lengthways along the midrib and push the cut edges into damp compost; plantlets form along the whole length.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.
