Basil (Ocimum basilicum) care

Herbs & edibles · needs attention to keep

Last updated 24 September 2026

Basil (Ocimum basilicum) wants full sun — six hours minimum. Water it every 3 days. Keep the compost damp — basil wilts fast and hard — but water in the morning, at the base, so the leaves are dry by night. Pot it in rich, free-draining compost. A balanced feed at half strength every two weeks in summer, and repot every 6 months. Humidity: room humidity is fine, with air moving. The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to cats and dogs.

Watering a Basil

Water every 3 days. Keep the compost damp — basil wilts fast and hard — but water in the morning, at the base, so the leaves are dry by night.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

As much direct sun as possible. Supermarket basil dies indoors mainly because a kitchen windowsill gives it a fraction of the light it needs.

Humidity and temperature

Room humidity is fine, with air moving. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

A rich compost that holds some water but drains freely. The pot a supermarket basil comes in is far too small and far too crowded — split it into three or four pots on day one.

Feeding

A balanced feed at half strength every two weeks in summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 6 months. Basil is an annual. Rather than repotting, take cuttings through the summer and start new plants.

Common problems

  • Whole plant collapses within a fortnight of coming home

    Too many seedlings in one small pot, all competing.

    Fix: Split the clump into three or four pots on the day you buy it, and put it in the sunniest spot you have.

  • Black spots and stems going soft

    Fungal disease from cold, damp, still air.

    Fix: Water in the morning at the base, improve airflow, and keep it warm.

  • Flower spikes forming

    It is bolting; the leaves turn bitter afterwards.

    Fix: Pinch out the flower buds and the top pair of leaves regularly to keep it producing.

Propagation

Cuttings root in a glass of water within a week. This is the practical way to keep basil going year-round.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs, and edible.

The ASPCA lists Basil (Ocimum basilicum) as non-toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.
