Ponytail palm (Beaucarnea recurvata) care

Trees · easy to keep

Ponytail palm (Beaucarnea recurvata) wants bright light, direct sun welcome. Water it every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then leave it until the pot is completely dry — the swollen base is a water tank and a ponytail palm is happy forgotten for a month. Pot it in gritty and sharply drained — cactus compost. A weak feed two or three times a year, no more, and repot every 2 years. Humidity: indifferent; dry air suits it. Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

The ponytail palm — neither a palm nor a tree, but a member of the asparagus family with a swollen water-storing base and a fountain of thin strappy leaves. It is the most drought-tolerant plant in this catalogue after the lithops: a month without water is routine.

Watering a Ponytail palm

Water every 2 weeks. Water thoroughly, then leave it until the pot is completely dry — the swollen base is a water tank and a ponytail palm is happy forgotten for a month.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Full sun. A yucca indoors wants the brightest window in the house and will stretch and weaken anywhere else.

Humidity and temperature

Indifferent; dry air suits it. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Cactus compost, or ordinary compost cut with a third horticultural grit. The cane rots quickly in anything that holds water.

Feeding

A weak feed two or three times a year, no more. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot every 2 years. Every three years or so. They are top-heavy — use a wide, weighted pot.

Common problems

  • Soft, squashy base

    Rot. It has been watered too often.

    Fix: Cut away the rot if any firm tissue remains, dry it out, and repot in gritty mix. Often fatal — prevention is the only real answer.

  • Brown leaf tips

    Dry air or salts building up.

    Fix: Trim the tips and flush the pot with plain water occasionally.

Propagation

From the offsets that sometimes appear at the base of a mature plant. Seed is the commercial route; cuttings do not work.

A cutting taken in PlantParlour keeps its link to the plant it came from, so a propagation years later still traces back to the mother plant — which is the point if you ever sell or trade it.

Toxicity and safety

Non-toxic to cats and dogs.
