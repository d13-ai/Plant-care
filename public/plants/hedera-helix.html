English ivy (Hedera helix) care

Vines & trailers · needs attention to keep

English ivy (Hedera helix) wants bright indirect light; cool conditions. Water it every 6 days. Keep evenly damp. Ivy dislikes drying out completely and shows it by browning from the tips back. Pot it in standard compost with perlite. A balanced feed at half strength monthly in spring and summer, and repot once a year. Humidity: above 50%; dry heat brings spider mites. Toxic to cats, dogs and people — causes vomiting and, on contact, a skin rash in some people.

English ivy: the hardy climber from European woodland, sold indoors in dozens of leaf shapes and variegations. It is easy outdoors and awkward indoors, because central heating gives it spider mites within weeks. A cool bright room, or a shaded porch, is where it actually works.

Watering an English ivy

Water every 6 days. Keep evenly damp. Ivy dislikes drying out completely and shows it by browning from the tips back.

That cadence is the one PlantParlour sets for this species when you name it, and it is deliberately on the cautious side — a plant that asks a day early is safer than one that asks a day late. Log each watering and the reminder moves with the plant rather than with the calendar.

Light

Bright indirect light and, importantly, a cool room. Ivy is a hardy outdoor plant and does badly in dry central heating — a chilly porch suits it better than a warm lounge.

Humidity and temperature

Above 50%; dry heat brings spider mites. Central heating in winter is what turns a comfortable room into a dry one, and it is the usual reason an established plant suddenly browns at the edges or picks up spider mites.

Soil and potting

Ordinary compost with perlite. Ivy is not fussy about the mix, only about the air around it.

Feeding

A balanced feed at half strength monthly in spring and summer. Stop feeding in autumn and start again when you see new growth in spring — feeding a plant that isn't growing just builds up salts in the pot.

Repotting

Repot once a year. Every second spring. Trim it hard at the same time; ivy takes cutting back well.

Common problems

  • Spider mites, repeatedly

    Warm dry air. Indoor ivy is a magnet for them.

    Fix: Move it somewhere cooler and more humid, shower it regularly, and treat weekly for a month when they appear.

    Spider mites: how to read it on any plant

  • Leaves going dry and brown from the edges

    Dry heat or a pot that dried out.

    Fix: Water more regularly and move it away from the radiator.

    Brown tips and edges: how to read it on any plant

  • Variegated ivy turning plain green

    Too little light.

    Fix: Move it brighter and remove all-green shoots.

Propagation

Stem cuttings root in water or damp compost within a few weeks.

Toxicity and safety

Toxic to cats, dogs and people — causes vomiting and, on contact, a skin rash in some people.

The ASPCA lists English Ivy (Hedera helix) as toxic to cats and dogs (checked 24 September 2026).

If your pet has eaten a plant, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435, or the Pet Poison Helpline on (855) 764-7661. Both are open around the clock and may charge a consultation fee. Take the plant or its label with you.

This is general information, not veterinary advice. We don't test plants: what we say about toxicity follows the ASPCA's published plant list as it stood on 24 September 2026, and that list can change. "Non-toxic" means not poisonous — a pet that eats enough of any plant can still vomit, and fertiliser, pesticide or leaf shine on a plant can harm it. It also only helps if the plant is what you think it is. It covers cats and dogs only; ask your vet about rabbits, birds, reptiles or anything else, and a doctor about children. Read our terms.

Named cultivars and variegations

Hedera helix is sold under several cultivar names. Care is the same unless noted; variegated forms carry less chlorophyll, so they grow more slowly and want more light than the plain species.

Light, pot size and the time of year all move that number, so treat it as a reminder to go and check rather than a rule.
